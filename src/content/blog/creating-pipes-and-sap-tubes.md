I designed and created multiple modular pipes and sap tubes for an industrial and steampunk region of the world, deep underground in a cold and metal environment. These various pipes and sap tubes are one of the most intuitive ways to add decoration and visual appeal to this type of environment. As a 3D modeler who specializes in hard surfaces, I was most excited to take on this challenge.

### Design Process (officially not a plumber)

![](/images/blog/variants.png)

It would be easy enough to plop down a primitive cylinder in Maya and call it a pipe, but where’s the charm in that? So, my process began quite literally at the beginning of the pipe, imagining these pipes would connect to each other and to other environment pieces, which led me to… flanges and gaskets! I felt that overly exaggerated flanges would benefit the stylization that Project Meridian strives for. I also had fun playing with the silhouette of pipes as I experimented more with them, and it got funkier and chunkier the longer I went. I also experimented with a Lattice Deformer in Maya to draw out the silhouette of the one on the left shown above.

### Finding the right style

![This is a garden trellis, with small pipes that act like vines entangled and growing just like a plant](/images/blog/trellis.png)

Continuing down the artistic exploration of what pipes and sap tubes could be for Project Meridian, and throughout discussions with the team about what this level could be like, they used words such as “organic, living, and intertwining,” and that was really striking to me. I rethought what I was designing and how I could further stylize these props to be unique and combine the game’s true and unique theme of “Gardenur Punk” with inspirations stemming from the traditional “steam punk.” I came up with this prop, which is inspired by a pipe system growing through a trellis, with more of a take on organic shape rather than hard surface. This prop began the mindset of separating the designing process from anything else that I have done or seen before and setting the artistic stroke on the canvas for what Gardenur Punk could be.

### Technical Obstacles (They just kept “piping” up)

Some of the technical challenges I faced during this process were deciding how much poly these cylindrical shapes needed to remain rounded in the way that I wanted them to, while not being too high of a poly count for optimization purposes. Sometimes when I worked on them for too long and so closely, I would start to see the hard edges that no one else, let alone the player, would most likely ever see. After taking breaks from Maya, I would be able to reset my creative and technical vision for these pipes.

### Pipes vs. Sap Tubes

![](/images/blog/variedtextures.png)

My favorite part about this process was taking initiative to make duplicates of all my pipe pieces to make iterations of either solid brass or brass and glass combinations! This would be the deciding factor in the Pipe vs. Sap Tube debacle I found myself in. If they were solid bronze, they fit into the cool-toned metal environment as a good contrast and made an effective pipe, and not every pipe needed to haul sap. Some would need to be for water and air. So, by duplicating my one-material pipes, adding a second material slot used for the Master Material in engine to create the glass, I was effectively recycling the pipes and giving them multipurpose while they would visually look distinct and not too repetitive! This is an optimal part of the design process for Project Meridian, where every piece of the world is designed with intention, even down to the pipes on the walls!

### Final Boss: Sap Machines

![](/images/blog/ingamerenders.png)

All of these sap pipes and tubes lead throughout the entire level to somewhere. Sap is an energy source as well, so in order to draw out pure energy from the sap, I designed this sap machine that can siphon, centrifuge, and recycle sap back into the storage tank. It also has a full control panel that allows it to be controlled by various settings, like pressure and quality of sap, speed, etc. I dare you not to press the big button!

I am so proud of what these modular pipes could come together to do! Thanks for reading!
